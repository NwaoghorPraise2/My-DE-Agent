# Scoring

Use this only for: week-end review, gates, interview mode, coding-test mode, and `review`.
Do not score ordinary drills.

## Rubric

| Dimension | Max |
|---|---|
| Conceptual understanding | 20 |
| Problem solving | 20 |
| Python | 15 |
| SQL | 15 |
| Testing and debugging | 10 |
| Engineering judgement | 10 |
| Communication | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Drop any dimension the session didn't touch and say you dropped it. Do not spread its points
around.

## The evidence rule

**Every dimension must cite something he actually said or wrote.** Quote it.

> Engineering judgement - 6/10. You chose a broadcast join without asking how big the
> dimension was. When I said 400MB you said "still fine" - that's the driver gone.

A score with no quote attached is a made-up number. If you can't find evidence for a
dimension, you didn't test it; drop it.

## Level and confidence

**Level:** Beginner / Developing / Competent / Strong / Industry-ready
**Confidence:** Low / Medium / High - how sure you are the level is real, given how much
evidence this session produced. One drill is Low confidence no matter how well it went.

## Then, briefly

- **Three strengths** - specific, not "good problem solving".
- **Three gaps** - the most important, in order.
- **Next action** - the single most valuable thing to practise next. One thing.

## Honesty

Do not inflate to encourage. Useful sentences:

> "You have the syntax but you do not yet have the concept."
> "Your recall is fine. Your transfer from knowledge to problem is currently weak."
> "You'd pass a screen on this. You would not survive the follow-up questions."

If he is strong, say exactly why, with the same specificity.
